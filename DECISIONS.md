# Decisions

Each judgment call made while building v1, why it was made, and what to revisit. Numbers come from `docs/pipeline-report.json` and `docs/test-results.json` (both regenerated by the pipeline and tests).

## Defaults used because the kickoff questions were not answered

The brief says to proceed with these defaults and flag them. **All five are still open for you to confirm.**

| Question | Default used |
|---|---|
| Standalone file or deployed project? | Both. `npm run build` makes a static site (with a GitHub Pages workflow); `npm run build:single` makes one 1.9 MB HTML file. |
| Audience | Internal team demo |

| NPC count and style | 300 NPCs, low-poly flat-shaded |

## Data

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bounding box | S 25.7363, W -80.2244, N 25.7902, E -80.1645 (6 × 6 km, about 36 km²), **centered on Axis at Brickell Village** | **Expanded on request** from the v1 Downtown + Brickell box (5.2 km²) to take in the surrounding neighborhoods. This is well over the brief's 3–4 km². The result: 11,191 buildings, 15,936 road/path segments, and 4.9 MB of processed data (about 5.1 MB on disk), right at the 5 MB budget. A bigger box would need tiled data streaming. |
| Data source | v1: Overpass (overpass.kumi.systems). The expanded map (2026-11-20) used the **OSM API tiled fallback**, because all Overpass servers timed out. | `overpass-api.de` reset every connection from this build environment. The fetch script tries the main server, then two mirrors, then falls back to tiled downloads from the OSM editing API (sequential, 1.2 s apart, cached). The fallback was written but **not used** for the shipped data. |
| Projection | Equirectangular around the box center (25.7705, -80.1895), 1 unit = 1 m | At this size the distortion is far below 1 m. |
| Simplification tolerance | 0.5 m (Douglas–Peucker) | As the brief suggests. Water and parks use 1 m / 0.5 m. If simplifying makes a ring self-intersect, the unsimplified ring is kept. |
| Minimum building area | 20 m² (landmarks exempt) | As the brief says. 6 buildings dropped. |
| Buildings outside the box | Dropped when the footprint centroid is outside (21 dropped) | Otherwise they float over the water past the clipped coastline. Roads and rail are clipped to the box. |
| Building parts | If a building's `building:part` pieces cover 50% or more of its area, the parent outline is not drawn (71 parents) but still blocks movement | This follows the OSM Simple 3D Buildings convention without double-drawing. |
| Open structures | `building=roof/canopy/carport` (29) are drawn as raised slabs and do not block movement | One of these stood on a Bayfront Park footpath and blocked the walking test. |
| Coastline → land | Coastline ways are stitched and clipped to the box, then closed along the box edge (OSM rule: land is on the left) | This gives 2 land polygons (mainland and Brickell Key) with no loose ends. The Miami River comes from `natural=water` polygons, which are cut out of the ground texture. |

## Heights

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Height order | `height` tag, then `building:levels` × 3.5 m, then a default | As the brief says. Of the rendered shapes: **532 from `height`, 110 from levels, 196 defaults, 11 landmark overrides**. |
| Default height | 45 m within 600 m of either core point (Brickell Ave & SE 8th St; Flagler St & SE 1st Ave), 12 m elsewhere | This is a stylized guess, not data. Every default building is listed in `docs/pipeline-report.json` (`defaultHeightBuildings`) and has a red outline in `docs/map-preview.png`. **Decision for you:** 196 defaults is about 23% of shapes. Adding OSM `height` tags upstream would improve this more than tuning the defaults. |
| Landmark overrides | 11 heights in `data/landmarks.json`, plus 13 label-only entries | Heights are the widely published architectural heights (Wikipedia / CTBUH figures). **They were entered from published figures but not re-checked online during this build, so every entry is marked `"verified": false`.** As a cross-check, 7 of the 11 agree with OSM's own `height` tag within ±6 m. |
| Large landmark disagreements | Four Seasons Hotel Miami (OSM 57 m vs 240 m), 1450 Brickell (144 vs 179), Freedom Tower (58 vs 78) | The OSM value probably belongs to a podium or omits the spire/crown. **Decision for you:** check these three first. |
| Brickell Flatiron | Label only; OSM name is "Flatiron Building" with a default 45 m height | I was not confident enough in a published figure to override it. |

## Rendering

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Building chunks | 200 m × 200 m, one merged mesh per chunk (106 chunks) | Lets frustum culling and LOD work per chunk while keeping draw calls low (77–150 measured). |
| LOD | Beyond 500 m from the player, a chunk swaps to minimum-area boxes; buildings under 8 m are left out | As the brief says. |
| Windows | Procedural floor/window bands in the shader, fading out between 350 and 900 m | No textures and no extra geometry. An `InstancedMesh` per window would cost much more for the same look. |
| Ground | One canvas texture at 1 px/m (land, parks, beaches) with the river cut out as transparency | Avoids z-fighting between overlapping flat polygons. Roads and paths are separate flat meshes. |
| Water | One flat plane at -0.6 m with a gray seawall strip along the edges | This is the "flat, simple shader" from the brief. There are no waves or reflections. |
| Sky and time of day | Fixed mid-afternoon sun from the south-west | Day/night lighting was not in scope. The simulated clock drives only NPC behavior. |
| Trees | 1,499 OSM tree nodes as instanced low-poly trees (about 55% palm-like, chosen at random) | OSM has no species data here. |

## Graphics upgrade (requested after v1: tiers 1 and 2)

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quality presets | **Low** = the original v1 look. **Medium (default)** = PBR materials, procedural textures, road markings, sky, reflections, sun shadows (2048 map), street props. **High** = Medium plus a 4096 shadow map, ambient occlusion, subtle bloom and a higher pixel ratio. | The 60 FPS target can't be measured here, so slower laptops can drop back to Low. The choice is saved in the browser; the G key or the start-panel buttons switch presets (the page reloads). |
| Tier 1 included | Shadows, sky and reflections were added along with tier 2 | You approved tier 2. Textures and props look flat without lighting, so the tier 1 lighting went in too, behind the presets. Say if you want it removed. |
| Textures | Generated in code at load (tileable noise: asphalt, concrete, ground detail, facade grime, water normals) | No downloads, no licensing questions, and no size impact. |
| Facades | Towers (70 m and up) get curtain-wall glass. Shorter buildings get punched windows; those under 25 m get ground-floor storefronts. Glass is smooth and metallic, so it reflects the sky. | This is still procedural, not real facade data. |
| Road markings | Drawn in the road shader: double yellow center line and white edge/lane lines on primary/secondary/tertiary roads, dashed white center line on local roads 6 m or wider, zebra stripes on OSM crossings, slab joints on sidewalks | Built from road type and width. They don't follow real lane counts or turn lanes. |
| Lighting | ACES tone mapping at exposure 0.5, sun 5.5, sky environment 0.45, hemisphere 0.5 | Tuned by eye on software-rendered screenshots. **May need retuning on a real display.** |
| Shadows | One shadow map, about 640 m square, that follows the camera's view and snaps to texels | Cheaper than cascaded shadows. Distant buildings have no shadows. |
| Trees | OSM tree nodes. About 55% are palms (curved trunk, 9 fronds) and the rest are shade trees, chosen at random. | OSM has no species data here. |
| Street lamps | 41 from OSM, plus **701 generated** every 38 m along both sides of primary/secondary/tertiary roads | **Decorative, not from data.** OSM has very few lamps mapped here. |
| Benches, bus shelters | From OSM bench (39) and bus-stop (100 placed) nodes. Shelters are set back from the nearest road edge, facing it. | |
| Parked cars | **500 generated** in curb lanes of residential, tertiary, secondary and unclassified streets 7 m or wider. None within 6 m of a crossing road, 5 m of a crosswalk, or 18 m of a bus stop. | **Decorative, not from data** (the brief allowed static parked cars). They block the player and NPCs. |
| Prop culling | Props are split into 250 m chunks (241 instanced meshes). Chunks farther than about 650 m (more when viewed from above) are hidden. | The first version, without chunks, drew about 1.5M triangles per frame. |

## Expanded map (on request)

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Center | Axis at Brickell Village (25.76323, -80.19443). It is the coordinate origin and has a label. | Requested. |
| Spawn | Still Bayfront Park | It's a recognizable open space. Use the map (M) to jump to Axis or anywhere else. |
| Neighborhood labels | Little Havana, Overtown, The Roads (approximate centers), plus loanDepot park, Lyric Theater and Máximo Gómez Park from OSM | Neighborhood label points are approximate and marked as such in `data/landmarks.json`. |
| NPC homes | 75% within 1.8 km of Axis, the rest anywhere. Trips go to destinations within about 1.5 km when possible. | 300 people spread evenly over 36 km² would be too thin to notice, and cross-city trips made pathfinding cost spike (up to 16.5 ms per frame at 400 NPCs). | Scale knobs | 300 m building chunks, ground and collision raster capped at 4096 px (about 1.5 m per pixel), fog 1.5–8 km, view distance 9 km, up to 3,000 lamps and 1,500 parked cars | Keeps draw calls under 700 even on High. |
| Coastline | Islands crossing the map edge (PortMiami, Watson Island, Virginia Key) are traced from a point outside the box | Fixes PortMiami rendering as water. |

## Traffic and crowd (added on request)

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Car models | Kenney Car Kit (CC0), 12 models, about 2,000–3,000 triangles each, all sharing one texture | Free, public-domain, and small (about 2.6 MB in total). The shared texture lets every car draw through one `BatchedMesh`, with per-car frustum culling. Photo-real car models would need paid or attribution-licensed assets and many more triangles. |
| Moving traffic | A bubble of cars within 650 m of the camera (300 / 600 / 900 by preset). Car models load after the first frame. Cars more than 850 m away move to roads 250–700 m away, out of sight. Cars beyond about 450 m are hidden. | Dense traffic wherever you are, at a fixed cost. |
| Driving rules | Right-hand lane offset of a quarter of the road width (between 1.6 and 4.5 m). Speed by road type (5–24 m/s). Cars brake for the car ahead and pause 0.8–3.3 s at 25% of junctions. Turns favor going straight. | Simple and cheap. **Not modeled:** traffic lights, one-way streets (the data doesn't keep the `oneway` tag), lane changes, or reacting to pedestrians. |
| Parked cars | Same 1,500 curbside spots as before, now with car or SUV models. Hidden beyond about 320 m. | |
| People | Ambient crowd within about 350 m of the camera (600 / 1,500 / 2,500 by preset). People walk the walk graph, picking a direction at each corner, with no route planning. The number outdoors follows the clock (about 5% at 3 AM, 100% at noon and 6 PM). | Replaces the routine-based crowd, which planned long A* routes across the whole map. Thousands of people now cost almost nothing at load, and wherever you are looks busy. The trade-off: people no longer commute between specific homes and offices. |
| Styles | Normal, Miami Vice, Noir. Each changes the sun, sky, fog and lights, then a final color-grade pass (plus bloom for Miami Vice). Photoreal gets the grade only. | Live switching with no reload. The grade runs after tone mapping, on display colors. |

## Build any area (added on request)

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | Entirely in the browser: data download, then the existing pipeline in a Web Worker, cached in IndexedDB | No server to run or pay for. The same code builds the prebuilt Miami data. |
| Data source | Overpass (40 s timeout), then the OpenStreetMap API in about 650 m tiles, plus up to 60 multipolygon relations | Overpass was often unreachable or slow from the build machine. The OSM API answered reliably and allows browser requests. Each build makes 15 to 100 requests, one at a time. Heavy public use would need a self-hosted extract instead. |
| Area size | 1, 2, 3 or 4 km square (default 2) | Keeps downloads under about 50 MB and builds under a minute. |
| Default heights | Median of the area's tagged building heights (6 to 30 m); 8 m if fewer than 20 are tagged | The Miami "core points" don't apply elsewhere. This avoids 45 m suburban houses. |
| Landmarks | Up to 7 tallest named buildings (25 m or more), then named buildings with a Wikipedia or Wikidata link, 10 total. Plus the 4 largest named parks and up to 4 neighborhood names. | A reasonable automatic version of the hand-made Miami list. Labels now keep a constant on-screen size. |
| Busyness | 0.8 × (floor-area ratio / 1.6)^0.6, plus up to 0.3 from restaurants and cafés per km², clamped to 0.2–1.3. It multiplies crowd and traffic size. | Chicago Loop → 1.3 (1,950 people, 780 cars); Oak Park → 0.47 (705 people, 282 cars). Miami's prebuilt data stays at 1.0. |
| Start | Flying at 110–320 m (scaled to the tallest building), 30% of the area south of center, looking north | Gives an immediate overview of any area. |
| Explore page look | joeferber.com tokens: Inter, navy ink, #533afd accent, white card with a soft float shadow | Matches the site it's hosted on. |

## Movement and collision

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creative flight (added on request) | Double-tap Space toggles flight (350 ms window, Minecraft's 7 ticks). Space climbs, Shift descends, double-tap W flies fast. Flight speed 11 m/s, fast 30 m/s, climb 8 m/s, ceiling 800 m. | Follows Minecraft creative controls. Speeds are scaled up for a 5 km² city: Minecraft's own fly speed is about 11 blocks/s. |
| Shift on the ground | Still sprints when walking; descends only while flying | Kept the v1 walking control. Minecraft uses Ctrl to sprint, but **Ctrl+W closes the browser tab**, so Ctrl is not used. |
| Speed multiplier (added on request) | + / − keys or mouse wheel, steps 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 8, 12×, applied to walking and flying, saved in the browser | Covers both slow street-level browsing and crossing the district quickly. |
| Height-aware collision | Every footprint has a height range. It blocks only where it overlaps the player's body (feet to 1.8 m above them). Anything at most 0.5 m above the feet can be stepped onto. | The same rule handles walking, flying beside towers, landing on roofs and walking across them. It also gives a real 0.5 m step-up, replacing the "flat ground" shortcut from v1. Invisible parent outlines (whose parts are drawn instead) block only at street level and can't be stood on. |
| Landing | Descending onto a roof or the street ends flight. Over water, flight can't end; you hover 0.6 m above it. | There is no swimming in v1. |
| World edge while flying | Clamped 1.5 km beyond the modeled area | Keeps you from getting lost over empty bay. |

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Speeds | Walk 1.6 m/s, sprint 6 m/s, jump 3.2 m/s (about 0.5 m peak) | Typical walking speed. Sprint is fast so the 5 km² area is quick to explore. |
| Collision | Exact footprint polygons in a 20 m grid, player radius 0.35 m; circle push-out gives sliding along walls | The brief requires sliding. Tested result: 4.16 m slid along a wall when 4.16 m was expected. |
| Water | 1 m raster of land minus water, plus bridge roads painted back in as walkable | This makes the Brickell Avenue bridge over the Miami River walkable. |
| Ground height | Street level is flat at 0 m; curbs are visual only. Since the flight update, the player can step up to 0.5 m and stand on roofs. | **Not built:** real curb heights or terrain. |

## NPCs

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Walk graph | OSM footways, paths and pedestrian ways, plus non-motorway roads (12,273 nodes, 14,189 edges). The largest connected piece is kept. | Sidewalk mapping alone was patchy. On road edges, NPCs walk offset to their right by half the road width plus 1.6 m, which puts them on the sidewalk side. Footway edges cost 1.35 times less than road edges in A*. |
| Edges through buildings | 319 edges dropped | NPCs follow the same walkability rule as the player. |
| Destinations | Cafés/restaurants (112), benches (29), bus stops and stations (121), park interiors (1,150 nodes), office-like buildings (80), residential buildings (81) | Snapped to the nearest graph node. "Home" is only residential buildings inside the box, so some commutes look short. |
| Routines | 5 archetypes (commuter 32%, office worker 20%, park visitor 22%, late-night worker 12%, tourist 14%), each with a ±36 min random offset | These are the brief's examples. "Inside" activities hide the NPC (they went indoors), so street crowds thin out at night. |
| Update budget | The 50 nearest NPCs get separation steering and limb animation every frame. The rest move every 4th frame, in bulk. | As the brief says. |
| Walking pace vs. clock | Near NPCs walk at 1.0–1.5× real pace. Distant NPCs move up to 10× when the clock runs fast. | At the default 60× clock, a 1 km commute at true speed would take 13 sim-hours. Distant bulk movement keeps routines coherent while nearby walkers look natural. |
| Default clock | 8:00 AM at 60× (1 real minute = 1 sim hour). Speeds 1× to 600× and pause. | Fast enough to see routines change within a few minutes. |
| Bodies | One instanced mesh per body part (6 draw calls for the whole crowd), 5 body proportions, 10 shirt / 6 pants / 6 skin colors | No skeletal animation, as the brief says. |

## Tooling

- Three.js 0.170, Vite 6, plain JavaScript (ES modules). No TypeScript, to keep the build to one step.
- `earcut` was not added as a separate dependency, because Three's `ShapeUtils` already bundles it.
- Tests use the Playwright and Chromium preinstalled in the build environment, so neither is a project dependency (see README).
